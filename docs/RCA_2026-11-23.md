## 1. The piano (first finding)

The piano is **Meteorites**, not the S.P.Y outro. In clip B it starts at the blend tick, **0:11** (11.03 s), which is Meteorites **bar 48** (1:06 into that track), the first bar of its half-time section [48, 61]. Mid-band correlation of the first 8 bars after the tick is 0.24 against Meteorites bar 48 and 0.02 against S.P.Y bar 240. The source spectrogram shows the same mid-range harmonic stack appearing at Meteorites bar 48 and missing from the S.P.Y outro. `FADE=long` raises the incoming to a 0.45 plateau in two bars, so the figure is clearly audible by **0:14** (clip bars 241–242 / Meteorites 49–50).

Why it feels unmotivated:

- We *start* the incoming track at that half-time piano. There is no phrase of Meteorites before it in the mix, and no drop context: drop 1 is 16 bars later and lands only as the blend ends (cue 48 + 16 = 64).
- Bar 48 is also eight bars after a mid-band rise at Meteorites bar 40, so the cue can sit mid-phrase of a line that already started.
- The other deck is a thin S.P.Y outro (lows already ~10 dB under body after drop 2 ended at 224), so the piano arrives over a fading record with no setup.
- Engine keys for this pair were `None→9B`, `key_ok=false` (S.P.Y has no Camelot entry). Harmony may also be unmoored, but the placement is enough to make the entrance feel random.

The pro does use that piano, but not as a cold open: Joe Syntax cues Meteorites at **bar 28** and overlaps ~33 bars (gone ~156, end of drop 1 into the breakdown). The half-time piano at bar 48 arrives about 20 bars into an already-running blend, during the outgoing breakdown, which is why it is motivated there and random here.

`epg_drop` does **not** fix this. On this pair it picks `["n", 224, null, 48, 16]` (last-drop-end exit, **same cue 48**). `epg_strict` stays on the default `["n", 256, null, 48, 16]`. Both still start Meteorites in the half-time piano.

## Ranked causes

### 1. The STRUCT outro-over-intro bonus cues Meteorites at drop1 − 16 = bar 48, which is the start of its half-time piano (root cause)

`mix_simple.pair_cands` gives a 300-point `STRUCTW` bonus to the window whose outgoing end is the last legal 16-bar-grid end of the track (256; `e_max` from music_end 259) and whose incoming start is `drop1 − X`. For X=16 that is cue **48**. That bar is exactly the start of Meteorites' half-time section [48, 61]. Cost of this window: **−178.8**. The next outro-over-intro option, `["n", 256, null, 32, 32]` (piano arrives 16 bars in, drop still lands at the end), costs **−87.2** and loses only because `length_pref` is 16. Cue 64 at the same exit (drop in the blend, piano skipped) costs **+94.6**, rank 36 of 328.

`hint_weight` 0.15 cannot move this: the STRUCT bonus is 300 points.

### 2. Drop-1-end and breakdown-end exits are generated, but they cannot beat the 300-point bonus

Legal candidates include exit 128 (end of drop 1) and exit 160 (end of the breakdown / start of drop 2), with cues 16/32/48. The closest to the pro (exit ~156, cue ~28) is around exit 160 / cue 32, cost **+93 to +256**, ranks 35–302. They are 270+ points behind the bonus window. The picker *did propose* them; it did not *prefer* them.

`EXITPOS` / `epg_strict` / `epg_drop` / `epg_stack` (nightly promotion arms, off by default):

- `epg_strict` (`EXITPOS` + `EXITPOS_GATE`): still `["n", 256, null, 48, 16]`, score 19.81. The gate keeps the default unless a post-drop earlier-outro alternative wins; here it does not.
- `epg_drop` (`EXITPOS` + `EXITPOS_DROP`): `["n", 224, null, 48, 16]`, score 26.04. This is last-drop-end (drop 2 ends at 224), which the listener preferred in an 11-19 ours-vs-ours A/B, but the **cue is still 48**, so the half-time piano still opens the blend.
- `epg_stack`: same drop window as `epg_drop` on this pair, score 26.79.

So the exit-position work moves the *outgoing* exit; it does not move this incoming cue off the half-time downbeat.

`SLOWDOWN=1` would not have fired either: it only penalises starting a blend inside the *outgoing* outro or in-drop slowdown. S.P.Y's slowdown is the breakdown [129, 162], not the outro 240–256. There is no existing cost for cueing into the *incoming* half-time section.

### 3. The 9.3 dB low-end dip is a symptom of 1, and the thin-window flags were off

Both decks in the middle of the default window sit more than 6 dB under their own body (S.P.Y outro 10.3 dB, Meteorites half-time 9.0 dB). The handoff is two quiet records. `THIN_DROPCUE` and `STRUCT_THIN` were built for this on 11-19 and are off by default, so the bonus stayed on the thin 48-cue window.

### 4. Why the other daily knobs did not move this window

- `FADE=long` *did* apply: 2-bar rise to 0.45, plateau, then the exit (bass swap 243–248, `TAIL_EXIT` 6 pulls the outgoing cut to bar 254). That is why the piano is audible so soon after 0:11. The shape cannot change the cue.
- `BLEND_LOCK` did not apply: "inconsistent / too few agreeing bars."
- `HIGH_SWAP` / `HIGH_OFS`: no high swap. `high_at()` needs both decks drumming; this outro-vs-half-time window is not beat-on-beat.
- `window_planner` 1: mid-track windows are nominated, but the 300-point bonus still wins; this pick stayed 256/48/16 (score 29.77, same window).
- `linger_pen_w` 0.4: same window, 29.77.
- `length_pref` 32: *did* move the pick to `["n", 256, null, 32, 32]` (score 33.06). The piano then arrives 16 bars into the blend, which would give it context. It overfit the held-out set (34.07 → 32.13) and was reverted.

### 5. Not a beat trainwreck on the clip he heard

After the (unapplied) lock, the offset median is −19.6 ms. The half-beat check is formally ok but has too few drum-on-drum bars to be a strong pass. The failed pro-lock and the 21.5 s scorer lag are the placement miss in cause 1, not a missed half-beat on this render.
