## How common the shape is

33 admitted corpus pros, same outgoing/incoming relative curves as the this-morning tail research.

- Incoming snap (-18 dB to -6 dB in at most 2 bars): **17/33** (52%). A 1-bar hard snap: 3/33.
- Shallow outgoing after that entry (at least 8 bars still between -18 and -6 dB): **24/33** (73%).
- Both together: **10/33** (30%), of which 7 are train and 3 are held-out. Six of the ten are Hybrid Minds, including today's pick.

So the gesture is common, not a one-off.

## The X1 guard

X1 caps the designed incoming rise at 3.0 dB/beat (floor -24 dB). Today's pro's steepest bar is 4.38 dB/beat, over the cap. A smooth two-bar rise to the same near-full level is 2.66 dB/beat, under the cap. Across the corpus, 1-bar incoming rises that start above -40 dB (silence-floor jumps dropped) have median 1.8 dB/beat and p95 **4.62 dB/beat**; only 4/26 exceed 3 dB/beat. A later calibration could raise the cap to that p95; this prototype does not. If a caller asks for a 1-bar slam, the rise is clamped to 3.0 dB/beat and the clamp is recorded.

A single equal-power fader also cannot drop the outgoing faster than 3 dB/bar. After the swap, moving from x=0.65 (incoming about -1.4 dB, outgoing about -5.6 dB, the top of X1's legal swap window) down to a -12 dB outgoing hold therefore takes about 2.3 bars, not a slam.

## Prototype: `SNAP_IN=1` (`src2/snap_in.py`)

Off by default. Post-pass on the one monotonic equal-power x(t):

- Delay the incoming rise until the last legal moment before the bass-swap centre.
- Rise at most 2.95 dB/beat incoming and 2.75 dB/bar outgoing, so the swap centre still sits at x in [0.2, 0.65].
- After the later swap centre, continue to a hold with the outgoing at -12 dB (`SNAP_IN_DB`, default 12).
- Hold through the original window end, then a short residual (about 4.4 bars at 2.75 dB/bar, cap 8) so the hold is not chopped to silence. 32-bar FADE=long solos keep their early exit and are not extended.
- Ride, rate, X1, and swap-x guards are honoured, never loosened.

`TAIL_SHALLOW` can be stacked: when both are on, the residual uses the shallower `TAIL_SHALLOW` rate. 8 unit tests in `src/tests/test_snap_in.py`.

## Not a global rule: pick it from the tracks

listener: these moves are not rules. Sometimes blends are quick and sometimes they last ages, depending on the tracks. So `SNAP_IN` and the shallow tail stay options, off by default, to be chosen per pair from track features (intro length, how much the vocals or melodies would clash, bass content, energy) rather than applied to every blend.

On 32 of 33 admitted pros with a source locate (Wilkinson's clip is missing `track_t`; vocal stems are not cached on this crate, so "clash" is proxied by both decks sitting near their own body energy or high-hats):

- The snap-in + shallow-tail combo (what B does today) is common only when the incoming cue is already in a drop: **9/19** (47%) vs **1/12** (8%) when the incoming cue is still in the intro. The single most typical shape is outgoing breakdown into an incoming drop: **6/11** (55%) are the combo. Our planner's usual `drop>intro` window (outgoing still in a drop, incoming from the intro) almost never gets this combo from the pro (1/11). Today's pick is a `breakdown>drop`.
- Snap itself is more likely when the outgoing is already down in level or bass (11/16, 69%) than when it is still at full body (6/16, 38%). Incoming energy vs its own body barely moves the rate. Incoming bass a little closer to ready is only a small nudge.
- A long shallow outgoing tail is more likely when plenty of the outgoing track is still left (81% far from the outro vs 62% near it; both outro-section cues had no shallow tail). Incoming tracks with a longer-than-usual intro (7 pairs, intro longer than 48 bars) almost never get a snap (1/7) and always get a shallow tail (7/7) — small n, but it matches "it depends on the track."
- When both tracks are already near full body energy, the combo is less common (22% vs 43%). Stacking two full decks with a snap-and-hold is something the pros usually avoid.

So a global on-switch would be the wrong shape for intro cues and for outros that should just leave. The next step is a per-pair picker, not a default change.
