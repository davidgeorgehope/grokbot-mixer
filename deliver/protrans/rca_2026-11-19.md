## Ranked causes

### 1. The STRUCT outro-over-intro bonus cues the incoming 16 bars before its drop, so the drop never plays in the overlap (root cause)

`mix_simple.pair_cands` gives a 300-point `STRUCTW` bonus to the window whose outgoing end is the last legal phrase-grid bar of the track and whose incoming start is `drop1 − X`. Here that is exit 224 and cue **48**. Seconds To Midnight's drop1 is 64, so the 16-bar blend is the late intro and the drop lands on the cut.

Cue **64** (the drop) with the same exit is already in the engine's ordinary candidate set (`ib` runs 0, 16, …, 80). It loses only because it does not get the 300-point bonus. `hint_weight` 0.15 cannot save a better cue: the time hint is about bar 160, exit 224 is 64 bars late, and the hint cost is 0.3 against STRUCT's 300.

The bass handoff then sits at mix bars 213–218, which is incoming source bars 53–58, still about 11 dB under the incoming body (−29.5 vs −18.2). That is the thin, short fade in the top and third panels of the before figure, against the pro still riding both decks in the second panel.

### 2. The exit is forced early, so we never hold both tracks the way the pro does

The last legal 16-bar-grid end is **224**: `e_max` is the earlier of music_end (235) and `tail0`, then rounded down to a 16-bar boundary. Ghetto Blaster's lows only die naturally around bars 227–228, and the track runs to 235. The pro's cut/blend events (21 / 21) hold the outgoing past 224. We cannot end at 232 or 240 without breaking the phrase-grid or music-end cap.

The blend also starts too late in the outro to have any of A's body left. A's last bar within 6 dB of its body (`tail6`) is **200**. Bars 208–224 are already about 21 dB under (lows around −39, body −17.7). So even a "correct" 16-bar outro fade is two quiet records, not a drop riding into a drop.

### 3. The 6.7 dB low-end dip is a symptom of 1 and 2, and the existing "no bass hole" flags do not fire

This is not the 11-14 "full drop dumped into a silent intro" hole (that one was 22.5 dB, with the outgoing still at body). Here the outgoing is already thin, so the 11-14 classic gate (outgoing still within `GAP_DB/2` of its body, incoming more than `GAP_DB` under) has **0 bars**. The measured 6.7 dB is a quieter handoff in a already-quiet outro, plus the incoming still waiting for its drop.

`BASS_COVER` and `DROP_SWAP` both use "every incoming bar in the window is at least 6 dB under body." Bars 48–61 of Seconds To Midnight are 11 dB under, but bars 62–63 ramp to −23.7 and −19.9 (5.5 dB and 1.8 dB under). `all_thin` is false, `incoming_no_bass` is false, and `DROP_SWAP.recue` returns None. The flags never see that the actual swap (bars 53–58) is still in the hole, because the last two bars of the window are already climbing into the drop.

### 4. Not a beat trainwreck on the clip he heard

After `BLEND_LOCK`, the decks stay inside the ±50 ms band through the loud overlap (median −0.7 ms). The half-beat check is formally ok, but it has too few drum-on-drum bars to be a strong pass. The failed pro-lock and the 21.8 s scorer lag are section errors (cause 1), not a missed half-beat on this render.

### 5. Why the other daily knobs did nothing on this window

- `HIGH_SWAP` / `HIGH_OFS`: `high_bar` is null. `high_at()` requires `bob()` (both decks drumming for at least half the blend). This outro-vs-intro window is not beat-on-beat, so there is no high swap (`mix_simple.py`).
- `TAIL_EXIT` 6: recorded `changed=false`; the window already ends at 224, so the tail exit has nothing to extend.
- `family_prior_w` does nothing unless `prefer_family` is set (it is null).
- `length_pref` 16 is why `nat_short` beat `nat_long` today. Dropping it to 12 or 14 is what switched this pick to a 32-bar window (see prototypes).
- `FADE=long` *did* apply: 2-bar rise to 0.45, plateau, then the exit. That shape is why B is audible early and A lingers to 224. It cannot put the incoming drop into a 16-bar intro cue.

## Would safe-placement variant d have avoided this?

No. Variant d was run on this pair with the same flags as the 19 November eval (`PUMPF=1`, `LANES=1`, `TAIL6=1`, `GAPX=1`, `PAYPEN=0.3`, `SECPEN=0.1`). Joe Syntax is not in the 91-pair corpus-v2 dev set, so the risk model was fit on all 91 (out of domain; the musical-fit term was omitted because this pair has no corpus-v2 row).

- The default window's payoff term is already 0 (A's last drop, 144–192, is fully heard before the blend; the cue is *before* B's drop, so d does not count it as a skip even though the drop is not *in* the overlap).
- `TAIL6` caps non-default exits at bar 200, so d cannot even offer a different cue at end 224 (cue 64 at 224 is not a d candidate). The only end-224 window it will consider is the default itself.
- Lane simulation on the default window predicted only a **0.57 dB** V-dip (threshold 4.54), against a measured 6.7 dB, so the risk model called the default safe (risk 0.043, rank 1 of 132). The top alternatives ended at 192 (end of drop 2) with cues 48 or 32, slightly lower raw risk but a 0.2 payoff penalty, and they lost. `keep_default` is true.

d would have left this pair on the same window the listener heard. The lane sim missing the mid-swap thin region is the same class of miss as today's earlier Etherwood `__11` note: the planner cannot dodge a hole it does not see.

## Proposed next step (not shipped)

A useful flag would be narrower than "always cue the drop" and tighter than `DROP_SWAP`'s all-bars-thin gate: if the *bass-swap bars themselves* are still more than the existing 6 dB thin line under the incoming body, nominate (or prefer) the already-scored cue that puts drop1 inside the overlap, without moving the outgoing end and without skipping the outgoing's last drop. That is a per-pair gate, not a blanket length or STRUCT change. It would need its own held-out run before any default discussion. X1, `GAP_DB`, and F1/N1 stay as they are.
