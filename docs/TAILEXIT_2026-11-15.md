# Outgoing fade-out research, 15 November 2026 (ET)

the listener approved a shorter outgoing tail (2026-11-13: he spots ours in the blinds because the fade-out lingers longer than the pro's). Measurement, then two off-by-default prototypes. Neither changed `rules.json` or any test threshold. Neither beat the held-out default, so no blind A/B was cut.

## Sources

- [Vande Veire & De Bie, "From raw audio to a seamless mix: creating an automated DJ system for Drum and Bass," EURASIP J. Audio Speech Music Process. 2018:13](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8), [doi:10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8). Section 4.3: after the bass/treble switch, "the volume of the previous song is then gradually faded out until the end of the crossfade." Fade-out lengths are 16 or 32 bars, not a short dump. Open-source: [aida-ugent/dnb-autodj](https://github.com/aida-ugent/dnb-autodj).

## What the data actually shows

Same NNLS deck-gain path as the scorer (`tail_measure.py`, `tail_curves.py`), outgoing all-band level relative to its own 90th percentile, 33 admitted pairs.

Length to a -12 dB relative exit after the detected bass swap: pro median **18.5 bars** (n=20, IQR 8.5–26.5); ours already **3 bars** (n=25). Designed FADE=long fader hits -30 dB **6 bars** after the swap end. Raw length is not the problem — ours is already shorter.

Level-vs-bar shape after the swap (median rel-dB):

| offset | pro | ours (FADE=long default) |
|--------|-----|--------------------------|
| swap   | -3.0 | -7.3 |
| +4     | -8.5 | -19.1 |
| +8     | -10.1 | -27.1 |
| +16    | -13.8 | -33.5 |

Bars after the swap still in the audible zone (-18..-6 dB): pro median **14**, ours **4**. At +8 bars, 24/33 pros are still above -18 dB; only 8/31 of ours are. On the 2026-11-13 daily pair (`hybrid_minds_ukf__12`) the pro stays around -2..-4 dB for the whole window; ours is already at -25 dB four bars after the swap and silent by +8. The obvious 8-bar downward fader on ours is the "fade-out that goes on"; the pro often does not have that gesture.

NNLS can floor a muted deck around -7..-12 dB (`docs/PROCESS.md`), so absolute tails to -30 dB are often censored on the pro. The relative mid-level curve (0 to -18 dB) is the reliable part, and it agrees with the designed fader: we dump, they don't.

## Prototype A: `TAIL_EXIT=N` (shorter tail)

`src2/tail_exit.py`. Off by default. Pulls the outgoing under -24 dB N bars after the bass-swap end, still one monotonic equal-power control, never steeper than 2.75 dB/bar (under the 3 dB/bar gradual-tail rule). Ride, X1, and swap-x guards are honoured: if they trip, the target is pushed later, never loosened.

| variant | train | held-out | vs default held-out |
|---------|-------|----------|---------------------|
| tebase (default, FADE=long) | 32.60 | **33.84** | — |
| TAIL_EXIT=2 or 4 | 32.67 | 33.83 | -0.01 |
| TAIL_EXIT=6 | 32.72 | 33.86 | +0.02 |
| tfbase (FADE=tail) | 33.27 | 33.76 | — |
| FADE=tail + TAIL_EXIT=2 or 4 | 33.65 | 33.39 | -0.37 vs tfbase |

N=2 and N=4 were identical: every 16-bar pair was clamped (ride or bass-swap-x), and the exit only moved 1.25 bars earlier. N=6 was a no-op on 26/33 pairs. The +0.02 on N=6 is 11/12 held-out pairs flat. On the daily pair, N=2/4 scored -0.13 and N=6 scored -0.05. Must-pass outgoing-fader-tail: 0 fails on the default diagnostic (28/33 all_pass; the five L1/N1 fails are pre-existing half-beat / summed-lows issues, not this flag).

A shorter tail is the wrong direction, and the gradual-tail plus ride rules will not let a much shorter one through anyway.

## Prototype B: `TAIL_SHALLOW=R` (shallower residual, the shape the curves show)

`src2/tail_shape.py`. Off by default. After the exit starts, the outgoing may not fall faster than R dB/bar (R=1.0 matches the pro median of about 0.9 dB/bar). The blend end extends until the line reaches -24 dB instead of snapping to silence at the planned window end. Same guards, never loosened. 12 unit tests in `src/tests/test_tail_shape.py` plus the existing `test_tail_exit.py` all pass.

R=1.0 on the held-out cohort: train **34.73** (+2.13), held-out **32.48** (-1.36), n_fail=0. Classic overfit. The daily pair jumped 12.19 → 26.62 (stem and deck curves, not the event cut). Held-out swings were large in both directions (netsky 02 -14.9, pola 00 -11.5; netsky 23 +11.0, pola 02 +10.6). Extending a 16-bar window by ~13 bars of residual helps pairs whose pro really stays in, and hurts pairs whose pro already cut.

R=2.0 (still shallower than our 2.6 dB/bar dump, only a few bars of residual): train **33.25** (+0.65), held-out **33.55** (-0.29), n_fail=0. Closer, but still a loss. The daily pair went 12.19 → 20.34 (R=2) vs 26.62 (R=1). The remaining held-out gap is the residual length, not the first few dB of the dump.

## What to try next

- A short residual cap (4–8 bars past the window) rather than riding all the way to -24 dB. The overfit is from the long extension, not from lifting the mid-fade a little.
- Apply the shallow residual only when the designed exit already sits at the window end (typical 16-bar daily case), and leave FADE=long 32-bar solos alone.
- Carry-over already queued: later bass handoff when the incoming track has no bass in the window; then psychoacoustic consonance.

The flags stay off. Default engine and `rules.json` are unchanged.
